**On the ordering claim (important nuance):** the reviewer judged the live polling timeline to be CORROBORATIVE, not the primary proof — 0.5s/2.5s samples cannot by themselves exclude 'installer spawned first, then before-quit stopped the proxy'. What actually closes it: installUpdateAndRestart() is a straight-line stopStatusPoller() -> await stopProxyForShutdown() -> markShuttingDown() -> quitAndInstall(), and it returns {ok:true} ONLY after quitAndInstall() returns, so a received {ok:true} is itself proof both ran in that order; additionally a timed-out/degraded stop would have deferred quitAndInstall() by the full 15s withTimeout, whereas pm2.log shows the stop completing in ~1s, with exactly ONE 'Stopping app:litellm-nim' line in the whole window (no second stop from before-quit). Alternative causes excluded: not a crash (explicit stop RPC logged, and autorestart:true would have relaunched one), not the installer (its writes are 113s later).

**The two uninstrumented elements do not block AC#3, stated deliberately rather than by default.** markShuttingDown() is defence-in-depth — src/main/index.js:181-192's before-quit handler sets shuttingDown itself before doing anything, so even a broken markShuttingDown() could not produce the double-stop/quit-loop it guards against. stopStatusPoller() is pure teardown hygiene with no bearing on the proxy's restart behavior. AC#3 asks whether the BEHAVIOR holds, and it was externally observed in full: a genuinely running app-supervised proxy is cleanly stopped as part of the update; the update installs; the relaunched app does not auto-start it; it can be started again and serves traffic.
